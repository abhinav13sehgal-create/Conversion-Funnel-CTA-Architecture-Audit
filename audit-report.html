The finding, stated plainly

The brief's premise needs correcting The working assumption was that Groto ranks for informational content while competitors rank for high-intent ICP pages, and that the fix is to build ICP pages. The data says something different and more urgent. Groto already has the ICP pages. Seven service pages. Six industry pages including SaaS and AI. A pricing page publishing exact monthly rates — something Clay, NeuronUX, Lollypop and Parallel HQ all refuse to do. Two competitor-alternative posts, one of which already ranks. Thirteen case studies. Seventy-nine location pages.

The problem is not that the pages don't exist. It is that they are unreachable, unsupported, and unproven. Nothing links to them from the content that earns traffic. Twenty broken duplicate URLs compete with them for the same terms. And not one of the thirteen case studies meant to close the sale contains a single number.

1 · The bridge does not exist

Twelve buyer-intent blog posts audited by byte-offset analysis of the raw HTML. Zero in-body links to any service, industry, pricing or contact page. Every commercial link on letsgroto.com sits in the global nav or footer of a 3.8 MB document.

Eleken places 2–3 CTA boxes per post, each worded to the section above it. Superside places 10–25 exact-match anchors. Even Ramotion — the designated failure case in this analysis — manages 6–8.

2 · Twenty broken URLs are eating the real ones

/saas-design, /ai-design, /health-care, /finance, /e-commerce and fifteen more all render the Projects template with H1 = "Projects", regardless of what their title tags promise. All twenty remain in the sitemap. Google surfaces /artificial-intelligence — one of the broken ones — as a top result for the domain.

Three separate URLs currently compete for the concept "SaaS".

3 · There is no proof

Thirteen of thirteen case studies contain no outcome metric. The only two numbers in the entire set describe the problem and never resolve it. Groto's homepage schema claims a 2.3× conversion lift for IVHQ — and IVHQ has no case study page.

That same schema block advises buyers that "red flags are portfolios with no measurable outcomes."

What the competitive set actually proves

Site size is not the lever

NeuronUX runs 140 URLs and captures 7,387 commercial visits. Ramotion runs 1,320 URLs and captures 1,422. Groto runs 383 and captures 63. Concentration beats volume — the three highest commercial-share domains in the set (Thomas Digital 87%, Clay 69%, NeuronUX 68%) are all among the four smallest.

Self-ranking listicles are the category norm

Eleken, Superside, Clay, NeuronUX and Parallel HQ all publish their own "best agency" roundups and place themselves at #1 with a disclosed methodology. NeuronUX's SF listicle ranks on page 1 alongside Clutch. Groto has three such posts already written — none of which links to anything Groto sells.

Location pages work — pointed at the right places

Parallel HQ crosses 6 service types with ~21 US metros for ~150 commercial pages and converts 1,633 buyer visits from just 6,363 keywords. Groto has 79 location pages across two duplicate systems, aimed at Des Moines, Fort Myers, Norfolk, Lancaster and York.

The India-to-US lane is wide open

Lollypop's /ui-ux-design-agency-in-usa/ is the strongest India-to-US page in the benchmark. But the query "UI UX design company India for US clients" returns a LinkedIn post and three directories in its top five. No premium boutique has built this page — and it is the closest query in the entire map to Groto's actual structural identity.

PHASE 9C The bridge strategy

This is the highest-leverage work in the entire plan, and it requires no new pages. Groto already ranks for buyer-stage queries — those rankings currently dead-end.

The mechanism to copy — Eleken's, verified across 8 posts 1 · Post-intro CTA box immediately after the intro, before any educational content. "Looking for a website that works as hard as you do? We'll create it for you. [Book a call]"

2 · Topically matched inline links. Each section links to the one service or case study matching that section's topic — not a boilerplate footer set. The link fires at the moment the reader's context matches a vertical.

3 · Multiple micro-CTA boxes per long post, each worded to the section immediately above it. Their 8,700-word UX-research post carries three: "Let us find the UX gaps hurting your product's performance → Start your audit with Eleken" → /ux-audit-service, and "Build your MVP with research-driven UX design → Let's validate your ideas together" → /engagement/mvp-design-for-saas.

4 · Closing CTA restating the full value proposition.
The mechanism to avoid — Ramotion's, and Groto's is worse Ramotion reuses the same five generic anchors ("Branding", "Web design", "UX design", "Design system", "Web app development") on every post regardless of topic, and its only in-content conversion ask is a newsletter signup. Result: 12,205 informational visits producing 1,422 commercial. Groto has zero in-body links at all — the same failure mode, one step further down.

PHASE 9D Case study strategy

Zero of thirteen Not one Groto case study contains an outcome metric. The only two numbers in the entire set — "80% of users browsing on mobile" and "62% didn't scroll past section 2" — describe the problem and never resolve it.

Meanwhile Groto's homepage FAQ schema states: "Every metric on this site traces to a named client engagement, not an internal estimate" and cites "the IVHQ engagement produced a 2.3X conversion rate increase and 1.25X improvement in customer retention." IVHQ has no case study page. The one hard number the agency owns lives inside JSON-LD, invisible to any human reading the page.

The same schema block advises prospects to ask for "a case study in your product category with named metrics" and warns that "red flags are portfolios with no measurable outcomes." It is an accurate description of Groto's own portfolio.
Which is also the opportunity No competitor in this set has solved case studies either. Thomas Digital: 0 of 11 with metrics. Clay: 1 of 4. NeuronUX: 4 of 11 — the best in the set, at 36%. If Groto ships 13 of 13 with quantified outcomes, it holds a proof position nobody in the category currently occupies. This is the cheapest genuine differentiation available.

PHASE 6 Live SERP reality check

Ten buyer queries checked against live US search results. Groto appears in the top 10 for two — and both are blog posts. Zero commercial or service pages surface for any buyer query tested.

Groto does not rank for its own positioning The homepage title tag reads "Groto | UX for AI Products, Agents & Dashboards." A live search for "dashboard UX design agency" returns zero Groto presence. Eleken owns it outright with a dedicated page. There is no standalone crawlable page for AI dashboards, AI agents or AI copilots anywhere on letsgroto.com — that positioning exists only as answer text inside homepage FAQ schema.

Open lanes — where nobody strong ranks

Queries where the SERP is directories, LinkedIn posts, generic staffing content, or off-topic tools. These are the highest-priority build targets because the entry bar is structure, not authority.
